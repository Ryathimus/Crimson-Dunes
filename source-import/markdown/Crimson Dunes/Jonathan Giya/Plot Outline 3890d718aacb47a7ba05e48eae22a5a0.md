Sandersons 1st Law: Your ability to solve problems with magic in a satisfying way is directly proportional to how well the reader understands said magic.

Sandersons 2nd Law: Limitations > Powers

Sandersons 3rd Law: Expand what you already have before you add something new.

Want and needs and why they can’t have it.

Promise, **progress** towards that promise, payoff on that promise.

Signpost the type of progress.

Plot 1 (Char 1, 12yo):

- We start out in the Aboriginal point of view before the colonist come and learn how they live with magic.
- When the colonists come, Char 1 make friends with a 10yo kid (Char 2) and have some adventures.
- During these adventures, Char 2 experiences some of Char 1’s magic, and realizes they have magic as well.
- Char 2 gets found out and forbidden from seeing their Aboriginal friend.
- Char 1 is forbidden from teaching Char 2 their magic.
- Char 1 gets sick and Char 2 has a dilemma on being able to see their friend when they realize they’re dying along with a large number of the Aboriginals.
- Char 1 dies and Char 2 regrets not being able to be there, and to attempt to help with his newfound magic.

Magical Settler View:

We don’t know how it happened. Some speculate there were relations between us and them, others think it was one of our common diseases, smallpox or something, gone terribly wrong. Regardless of the how, we know now that our respective magics needed to go through some sort of cataclysm to bring both of our societies together. And while it absolutely devastated their population, the ones that survived joined with us to begin something... magical.

Magical Aboriginal View:

We called it The Plague, capital T and everything, but it was actually something more akin to magical biosynthesis. A melting pot between their magic and ours, which clashed so heavily that it killed off the majority of the indigenous mob.
